# Plain writing: the rulebook

The goal isn't to ban words. It's writing with a point of view, plain language, and no
padding: something a colleague would believe a person wrote.

---

## The top offenders

### 1. Hype and filler
Cut anything that warms up instead of saying something.

- No empty openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "As we all know", "Now more than ever".
- No "It's not just X, it's Y" constructions.
- No throat-clearing before the point. Start at the point.

> ❌ "In today's fast-paced incident response landscape, having the right tools is more important than ever."
> ✅ "Our on-call team loses an hour a week rebuilding the same dashboard. Here's how to stop that."

### 2. Overused AI words
Reach for the plain word instead.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|-------------|
| delve into | look at, dig into, cover |
| leverage | use |
| robust | solid, reliable, holds up |
| seamless | smooth, works without friction |
| elevate | improve, raise |
| unlock | enable, open up, let you |
| utilize | use |
| facilitate | help, make easier |
| streamline | simplify, speed up |
| a myriad of | many, lots of |
| cutting-edge / state-of-the-art | new, current |
| game-changer / transformative | (say what actually changes) |

If a word is doing PR instead of describing, cut it.

### 3. Hedging and wrap-ups
Say it once, with confidence, and stop.

- Drop "It's worth noting that", "It's important to remember", "Needless to say".
- Drop the summary paragraph that repeats what you just said ("In conclusion...", "To sum up...").
- Don't over-qualify. "This might possibly help in some cases" → "This helps when X."
- No fake enthusiasm. Skip the "Great question!" and the exclamation points that aren't earned.

> ❌ "It's worth noting that this approach could potentially help streamline your workflow in many cases."
> ✅ "This cuts the weekly export step from 20 minutes to one click."

## Secondary tics to watch

- **Em-dash overload.** A few are fine. If every other sentence has one, swap some for a period or a comma.
- **Everything-is-a-list.** Not every thought needs bullets. Use prose when ideas connect; use lists when items are genuinely parallel.
- **Rule of three.** AI loves "fast, simple, and reliable." Real writing varies. Use one strong word, or two, or four. Break the pattern.
- **Symmetrical structure.** Paragraphs that are all the same length and shape read like a template. Vary them.
- **Hollow transitions.** "Furthermore", "Moreover", "Additionally" stacked back to back. Often you can just delete them.
- **Restating the prompt.** Don't begin an answer by repeating the question back.

---

## What to do instead

- **Have a point of view.** Say what you actually think and why. A recommendation beats a balanced menu of options.
- **Be concrete.** Numbers, names, specifics. "MTTR dropped from 4h to 90m" beats "significantly improved resolution times."
- **Write like you talk.** Read it aloud. If you wouldn't say it to a colleague, rewrite it.
- **Lead with the answer.** Put the conclusion first, then the support (BLUF: bottom line up front).
- **Cut ruthlessly.** If a sentence can go without losing meaning, it goes.
- **Let it be uneven.** Short sentence. Then a longer one that earns its length. Rhythm signals a human.

---

## Quick self-edit checklist

- [ ] Does the first sentence say something, or just warm up?
- [ ] Any words from the "avoid" table? Swap them.
- [ ] Any hedging ("worth noting", "might possibly")? Cut or commit.
- [ ] Is there a wrap-up paragraph that repeats the body? Delete it.
- [ ] Could a colleague tell a person wrote this? If not, where does it sound canned?
- [ ] Read it aloud. Does anything make you wince?
