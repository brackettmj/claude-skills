# Plain writing

The rulebook is `references/rules.md`. Read it before you start. It lists what to cut
(hype, the overused-word table, hedging, wrap-ups, the secondary tics) and what to do
instead (lead with the answer, be concrete, let the rhythm be uneven).

Pick the mode from the request. "Write me…" or "rewrite this…" means **draft**.
"Check this", "what's wrong with this" or "does this sound like AI" means **review**.
If it's unclear, review first and offer the rewrite.

## Draft mode

1. Find the point before writing a word. If you can't say it in one sentence, ask.
2. Put that sentence first. Support comes after.
3. Use the user's specifics: their numbers, names, dates. Where a sentence needs a fact
   you don't have, leave a marked gap like `[number of incidents]` rather than writing
   a vague phrase to cover it.
4. Write it, then run the self-edit checklist at the end of the rulebook against your
   own draft before you show it. Your first draft has tells too.
5. Hand back the text only. No preamble ("Here's a plainer version!") and no notes
   about what you changed, unless they asked for them.

## Review mode

Don't rewrite the whole thing unless asked. Mark what's wrong so the writer learns
the pattern.

1. Quote each tell exactly, with its location if the text is long.
2. Name the category from the rulebook: filler, overused word, hedge, wrap-up, or the
   specific secondary tic.
3. Give the plain fix for that line.
4. Order by impact. A throat-clearing first paragraph matters more than one "robust."
5. End with the one or two habits that show up most, so the writer has something to
   watch for next time.

Don't flag things that aren't problems. One em dash is fine, a list of parallel items
is fine, and a technical term with no plain synonym stays. A review that marks every
other word teaches nothing.

## What this is not

- **Not a detector.** It improves writing. It can't tell you whether a model wrote
  something, and it shouldn't claim to.
- **Not a spelling or grammar pass**, unless the user asks for one.
- **Not a voice transplant.** Keep the writer's own vocabulary and opinions. Take out
  the padding, not the person.
